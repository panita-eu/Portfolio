Case Study — 01

BMS Transaction
Redesign

Five of BMS's core document flows, rebuilt from a one-line brief into a shared pattern every client deployment now inherits.

RoleUX/UI Design Team Lead
Scope5 modules · shared flow pattern
ConstraintShipped across multiple client deployments
Year2025
BriefAuditPrinciplesPatternBefore / AfterHandoffOutcome
02

Brief

The MD's ask arrived at business level: improve the visual design, tighten the UX, and shorten the flow. There was no formal spec and no user research attached. Translating that into concrete design decisions was the actual work.

BMS is a TMES product. This base design ships to client deployments, and TMES re-skins it to each client's own CI, so every decision had to hold up as the foundation each deployment is built from.

Design shown is TMES's default CI. Company logo is blurred. For each client deployment, TMES re-skins the colors, logo, and branding to the client's own identity.

03

Audit

I walked each existing form screen by screen, separating real business logic from things that were there by habit. Four patterns repeated across the first three forms I audited (Quotation, Purchase Request, Invoice), and Good Receive Note and Delivery showed the same ones.

A

Dead ends

The SKU List was a bare pagination bar with no table. Payment Method and Refer Sale Order / Delivery all read "No available options".

B

Irrelevant fields

The Purchase Request carried a full address block, Customer / Tax ID, and sales-style promotion, voucher and bill-discount fields.

C

Missing fields

No Requested or Due Date, no attachments, and no way to trace an invoice line back to its sale order.

D

Flat hierarchy

One long column, a single status toggle, Grand Total blended into the field list, and a two-button footer.

04

Principles

With no spec to lean on, I turned the audit into four rules the team could check any screen against.

  1. No dead ends. Every control either works or isn't shown.
  2. Relevant by default. Show what the document needs; optional fields sit behind "+ Add More Fields".
  3. Traceable. A line item can be followed back to where it came from.
  4. Save at any point. Long forms shouldn't be all-or-nothing.
05

Shared Pattern

The forms ran on the same skeleton with the same problems. Rather than patch each one, I fixed the skeleton once and applied it to every module.

1

Header

Document title and status. Quotation uses a five-step stepper.

2

Sectioned body

Branch, then party details, then SKU List, then Remark and Summary.

3

One SKU table

A single component with a variant per module (QT / PR / INV / GRN / DO).

4

Action bar

Back, Submit, Preview. Save draft on Quotation and PR.

06 / 01

Quotation

The clearest before and after. The old SKU List was a bare pagination control with no table, no search, and no way to add a line item on screen.

  • SKU List goes from a bare pagination bar to a searchable, editable table with Import Data and Insert below (28,978 records across 55 pages)
  • Status becomes a five-step stepper: Draft, Pending, Processing, Complete, Cancel
  • Summary regrouped, with optional fields behind "+ Add More Fields" and a Show Total In Words option
  • Save draft and Preview added; the old form only had Cancel and Create New
06 / 02

Purchase Request

The old form carried sales-style fields a purchase request doesn't need, and left a broken Payment Method field visible. The new one drops what doesn't apply and adds what procurement needs.

  • Header fields reduced from 16 to 6; the sales-style discount summary (12 fields) removed
  • Dead Payment Method field removed
  • Requested Date, Due Date and a photo attachment (JPG / PNG) added
  • Save draft and Preview added
06 / 03

Invoice

The old form couldn't trace a line back to its sale order: both "Refer" dropdowns sat empty. The new form makes that link explicit and separates what is owed from what is withheld.

  • The two empty "Refer" dropdowns become an Add Sale Order action with an SO Ref. No. on every line
  • Grand Total gets its own highlighted block, with Withholding Tax Amount and Net Amount to Pay beneath it
06 / 04

Good Receive Note

This is the receiving side of the warehouse: stock coming in against a purchase order. The old Receive Product form showed the same four problems as the others. The purchase order dropdown read "No available options", and a receiving document carried sales-style discount, promotion and voucher fields. The new form follows the receiving flow: who it came from, what was ordered, what arrived, and the proof.

  • The empty purchase order dropdown becomes an Add Purchase Order action, with a PO Ref. No. on every line that links back to the order
  • The eight-field address block is replaced by a Vendor Details section: pick the vendor and the address, contact, phone and email follow
  • The bare SKU List pagination bar becomes a searchable table with Import Data, Qty, Unit, VAT, WHT and Amount per line
  • A photo and document attachment area is added, with upload progress, so proof of receipt stays with the document
  • The 12-field summary (three kinds of discount, bill discount, VAT, WHT and shipping) becomes five lines and a highlighted Grand Total (counted from the designs)
  • Save draft and Preview are added next to Submit; the old form only had Cancel and Create New
06 / 05

Delivery

This is the outbound side: stock leaving against a sale order. The old form asked for a payment method and payment term on a delivery, and its Sale Order dropdown was empty. The new one splits the work into who it is for and how it ships, and shows what is in stock while the quantity is being entered.

  • The empty Sale Order dropdown becomes an Add Sale Order action, with an SO Ref. No. on every line
  • Payment Method and Payment Term are removed from the delivery; what a delivery needs is split into two tabs, Customer Details and Shipping Details
  • Shipping Details holds Delivery Date and Shipping Methods, with rarely used fields behind "+ Add More Fields"
  • Each line shows In stock under the Qty Delivered input, so the quantity is entered with stock on hand in view
  • The bare SKU List pagination bar becomes a searchable table with Import Data; Grand Total gets its own highlighted block; Preview is added
07

Review & Handoff

The MD reviewed and approved the designs. At handoff I walked the dev team through the shared pattern, so each module was built from the same structure instead of separate forms.

08

Outcome & Reflection

  • The Quotation SKU list became a working table
  • Quotation and Purchase Request gained Save draft
  • Purchase Request went from 16 header fields and 12 summary fields to 6 and none (counted from the designs)
  • Invoice gained line-level traceability to sale orders, plus Net Amount to Pay
  • Good Receive Note and Delivery gained the same traceability (PO Ref. No. and SO Ref. No. per line), attachments on receiving, and stock on hand shown while entering a delivery quantity

What I'd do next. I'd measure time-to-complete with real users and test the shared pattern with people from two client deployments, so the gains are measured, not just counted from the designs.

An idea I haven't designed yet. Receiving and delivery are the flows people do on a warehouse floor. If BMS needed a handheld version, I'd reuse the same document structure and SKU line, and change the interaction: scan to add a line, larger touch targets, and only the fields needed at the shelf. This is a direction, not delivered work.
